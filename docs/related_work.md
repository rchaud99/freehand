# Positioning: what Freehand can claim

This is a focused related-work check, not an exhaustive novelty review. Sources below are primary research abstracts checked September 28, 2026. Absence of a feature from an abstract is not evidence that a paper lacks it.

| Work | Relevant overlap | How to position Freehand carefully |
|---|---|---|
| [NewtonBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07172) | Interactive discovery of altered physical laws through experiments in simulated systems; noise and system complexity matter. | Hidden-law discovery and counterfactual environments are established ideas. Emphasize adaptation while accomplishing a persistent drawing task, with costly irreversible actions and next-target transfer. |
| [PhysGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15550) | Sequential probing of simulated physics, with controlled prior knowledge. | Disclosure and known-family ablations are not new in themselves. Make Freehand's observation and reference-access assumptions explicit. |
| [DiscoverPhysics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26087) | Agents propose experiments, observe trajectories, and submit explanations plus inferred laws; latent structure and refinement are central. | Do not present stateful dynamics as a new category of reasoning evaluation. A compact visual interaction loop and deterministic action scoring are the proposed distinction. |
| [SCILAWS-BENCH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01552) | Real-data law discovery and active querying of synthetic parallel worlds; predictive fit can differ from scientific validity. | A good drawing or plausible rationale is not proof of a correct causal model. Measure behavioral transfer and identifiable interventions separately. |

## Proposed contribution statement

Freehand studies adaptation to changing tool behavior during an ongoing visual task with persistent action consequences. It separates final completion from transfer to a new target, keeps failed actions in the interaction cost, and provides replayable simulation evidence plus controlled numeric-feedback comparisons. The September 28 extensions evaluate action-dependent memory and latent modes using explicit solvability and local-repair controls.

This is a proposed contribution, not a demonstrated claim of unique prior art. The current new experiments establish learnability and reference-agent separation on a specified generator. Frontier-model results, broader geometry, perception controls, and an exhaustive literature comparison remain required before a strong benchmark-paper novelty claim.
